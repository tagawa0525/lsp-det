# Desk research corpus of readiness vocabulary: mapping Serena's 70 servers onto this protocol

[日本語](readiness-vocabulary-corpus.ja.md)

ADR 0018 Decision A-5, ADR 0019 M8. A record of carrying out the proposal from the external review ([external-review-2026-09.md](external-review-2026-09.md) (Japanese) section 6): "map Serena's table of 30 languages onto lsp-det's vocabulary; if every cell fills in, the readiness vocabulary can be frozen. The rows that do not fill in become the list of places that must not be frozen." This is a supporting document for the upstream proposal, and it is also the basis for choosing "the languages to verify against real servers" in ADR 0019 Decision B.

## Method

- The starting point is the table in [serena-solidlsp.md](serena-solidlsp.md) (Japanese) section 1.2 (Serena `7fcbca7e`'s `src/solidlsp/language_servers/`: 44 dedicated classes plus 26 "other, immediate" servers = 70 servers)
- **Each row was checked against the server's own documentation and source** (ADR 0018 Decision C: do not infer a signal from how Serena waits). Sources cite the GitHub source (`gh api` / `gh search code`) and official documentation; where a source could not be reached, the row is marked "unconfirmed"
- The draft was split into 4 groups and delegated to Sonnet (2026-09-06); I then spot-checked 8 of the main claims against primary sources myself (at the end). The mapping and the "questioned" judgment in each row are based on that row's own source
- "Mapping questioned" means one of: `ready` cannot be said without spending time, progress is per-request / per-file and does not fit a single readiness value, it depends on a companion server, it can only be obtained by polling, the signal is disabled by configuration, or there is no signal and Serena's sleep suggests a real gap. "None" means either the mapping can be made without spending time, there is no doubt that no signal exists (so `unknown` is correct), or there is no indexing phase at all (so `ready` is correct)

## Summary

All 70 rows map onto one of `initializing` / `indexing` / `ready` / `unknown`, and **no row required a new value**. The substance of the 38 rows marked "questioned" is not a gap in the vocabulary but rather the structure of the signal (multiple tokens, per-request, configuration-dependent) and the absence of a signal (where `unknown` is correct).

Implications for the vocabulary:

- The four readiness values absorbed the signals of all 70 servers. They can be frozen
- What is needed is not a new value but three rules for the observer: (1) with multiple tokens, go to `ready` once none remain incomplete, (2) per-request / per-file progress is not turned into readiness, (3) the observer may inject the configuration needed to enable an opt-in signal. All three stay within the scope of the synthesis rules in chapter 8; the server's obligations (chapters 3-7) are unchanged
- A server that can only declare completion through time (Metals) is correctly `unknown`; time-based judgment is not brought in (ADR 0019 Decision C)
- Only rust-analyzer, gopls, jdtls, tsls (crashes), clojure-lsp, phpactor (watcher death), and Roslyn (a one-time toast) have roughly a health signal; most are `unknown`. For the health axis, "map it if it exists" is enough
